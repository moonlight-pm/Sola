Privacy

Sola is a desktop environment that runs on your own computer. Its Calendar application can connect to your Google account. This page says which Google user data Sola accesses, how it uses that data, and how it protects it. Calendar sends no analytics or telemetry.

Google user data Sola accesses

When you connect a Google account in Settings → Calendar, you sign in on Google's own page in your browser, and Sola asks Google for two permissions:

  • See and edit events on your calendars (calendar.events).
  • See the list of calendars you subscribe to (calendar.calendarlist.readonly).

With them, Sola reads:

  • From your list of calendars: each calendar's ID, name and colour, and whether you may change its events. Your primary calendar's ID is your Google address, which Sola uses as the account's name.
  • From each calendar's events, for the weeks around the dates you are looking at: each event's ID, title, description, location, Google Meet link, status, and start and end times.

Sola writes only what you do in Calendar: an event you create, change, or delete. It does not ask for or read anything else in your Google account, such as your mail, contacts, files, or profile, and it never sees your Google password.

How Sola uses Google user data

Only to show you your calendars and events: in Calendar, in the menubar clock's panel (the coming week), and in the reminders Sola shows before an event. And to create, change, or delete an event at Google when you do that in Calendar.

Your computer talks to Google directly. Nothing passes through a server of ours, and we never see your calendars, your events, or your sign-in. No person reads your data. Sola does not sell it, share it, use it for advertising, or build a profile from it. It sends your Google data to no one but Google.

Sola's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

AI and machine learning

Sola sends no Google user data to any AI or machine learning model or service, and none of it is used to create, train, or improve a model, ours or anyone else's.

Other parts of Sola work with AI: Bots talks to Grok, Claude, or Gab AI on your own account with each, and Projects and Design run coding agents you start. Sola gives none of them your Google data. Calendar offers no command or tool through which an agent could ask for it.

How Google user data is protected

  • Sign-in. You sign in on Google's page, never in Sola. Sola uses Google's OAuth 2.0 with PKCE, and Google returns the sign-in to Sola at an address on your own computer (127.0.0.1).
  • In transit. Sola talks to Google only over HTTPS (TLS), directly from your computer, and checks Google's certificates.
  • At rest. Sign-in tokens are encrypted on disk with age (X25519), using a key in ~/.config/sola/key that only your account can read. Calendar's copy of your calendars and events, and your reminders, are in files only your account can read.
  • Nothing of ours to break into. We run no server that holds your data, so there is no copy anywhere but your computer and Google.
  • Least access. Sola asks for the two permissions above and no more.

Where it is kept

On your computer, in your home directory. ~/.local/state/sola/calendar/ holds the calendars and events Sola last fetched, and your reminders. ~/.config/sola/state.yaml holds your connected accounts, with their tokens encrypted, and how each calendar is shown. ~/.config/sola/calendar/agenda.yaml holds the coming week for the clock's panel. Each refresh brings Sola's copy up to date with Google.

Other programs running under your account can read these files, as with anything in your home directory.

Removing it

In Settings → Calendar, open the account and choose Disconnect. That deletes the account, its sign-in tokens, its calendars, its events, and the reminders on them from your computer. If Calendar is closed, its copy goes when it next starts.

Disconnecting does not tell Google. To withdraw Sola's access there, remove it at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

iCloud and other calendars

An iCloud or CalDAV password is encrypted the same way and is used only to talk to that server. Calendar fetches a calendar URL directly from where it is hosted.

This site

sola.moonlight.pm sets no cookies and runs no script. Its fonts come from Google Fonts, so your browser asks Google for them. The server may keep ordinary request logs.
