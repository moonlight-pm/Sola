Keep them

AS DRAWN

The bar lists windows; Projects lists its agent tabs; the browser lists its pages. Each list stays in the app that owns its tabs. On one screen, though, that is three vertical strips in the first 1240 px, and they mean different things.

One list

THE DESKS IDEA'S END STATE

An app's own tabs hang under its window row. The browser and Projects publish their tab lists and accept "show this one"; both already have call planes that list tabs. While the bar is up, the apps hide their own strips. This is the expensive one.

Docked, rail, or summoned · desk x 0–410, 1:1
Against board 04
+You can see what is behind. Every window on the workspace, by zone, including the ones a zone is not showing. 04 only tells you which workspace you are on.
+Zones become stacks. Opening a window into a taken zone is fine: it goes on the stack and the bar lists it. Today a second window in a zone sits over the first, and nothing on screen says the first is there.
−It costs width on every workspace. 250 px docked. The rail costs 62; summoned costs nothing until you ask.
−A second way to switch. Super+Tab, Super+\` and the bar all move between windows. The bar has to be the one you reach for, or it is furniture.
Yours to pick
ADocked, rail, or summoned. Proposed: summoned from the workspace bubble, with docked as a setting. It keeps 04's top row and adds the tree only when you want it.
BKeep three lists, or one list. Proposed: keep them for now. One list is the desks idea's panel model by another road.
CWhich zones. Three thirds and Floating, as drawn, or the full numpad set (halves, top, bottom) as zone headers.
DA new window's zone. Onto the focused zone's stack, or the zone the app last used. Proposed: the app's last zone, then the focused one.
