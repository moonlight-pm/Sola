What it takes
Solar is a theme change, not a renderer change. Every surface stays opaque, nothing blurs, nothing new composites in River. The work sits in the places that assume the desk is always dark, and in one small scheduler that knows where the sun is.

Risks
GPU idleTiled windows must stay opaque so River can scan out: theme_for(false) marks the full opaque region from an opaque background.base (docs/architecture.md, "Tiled kit opaque-region"; crates/iced_winit-patched). Every Solar background is opaque, so this holds. Do not make the bar translucent to show a sky.
Mid-fade contrastBetween L 0.40 and 0.75 text contrast drops. Eased lightness keeps that to about six minutes per crossing fade; a "change at once" option skips fades entirely.
Status huesDawn's peach signal sits between danger and warning. Shape carries status as it does today, and dawn's danger leans crimson.
Theme churnAn emit restyles every window mid-task. iced restyles without relayout, but check that text inputs, selections, and scroll positions survive an emit.
An order that ships something each step
1The solar line, in Graphite. It needs no new light: the sun crosses a dark bar by day, the moon tick by night. Clock dropdown rows with it.
2A light-aware kit. is_dark, ink edges, chrome from the theme. Prove it with Day as a static preset in the storybook.
3Day by hand. Terminal palette, depth tokens, the shell's literals. Pick it in Settings like any theme.
4The scheduler and fades. Follow the sun, then dawn and dusk.
5Web pages. The CEF color scheme at the ink flip.
